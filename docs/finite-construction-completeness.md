# Finite construction completeness

First-shot perfection is not the acceptance condition. This additive derivation
uses the frozen 180 retained-native feature observations and independently
enumerates each source-bound four-path space against its eight declared cases.
It runs no prediction, compiler or generated Go process, and changes no raw receipt.

| Metric | Denominator | Current observations |
|---|---|---|
| Declared finite completion | All declared cases | 7 / 8 = 87.5% |
| Finite legal-space attainment | Maximum passes over the four legal typed paths | 7 / 7 = 100% |
| Irreducible declared-case gap | Declared cases, with finite legal maximum subtracted | 1 / 8 = 12.5% |
| Mean committed-attempt completion | Mean best finite completion after attempts 1..4 | 65.625% or 87.5% |

The legal maximum is recomputed using independently authored integer-state
oracles for every declared mask. It also matches the previously authored finite
best-set metadata. Complete native candidate enumeration, zero type rejection,
unattempted count zero and selected function AST reconciliation were already
required by the source audit. The selected result reaches that finite maximum.

**100% attainment does not mean all declared cases pass.** It means the emitted
body reaches the best attainable finite score inside these four authorized paths.
The remaining case is deliberately contradictory within this bounded space.
An irreducible gap cannot prove arbitrary-source or all-int64 impossibility, and
maximizing a finite contract cannot resolve natural-language intention ambiguity.

The progress curve records best completion after every committed candidate
attempt. Initial ranking and feedback-only checkpoints carry no new committed
attempt and are excluded. The best completion must be monotone, and its terminal
point must equal the native final receipt. The area here is an equal-attempt
arithmetic mean, not a wall-time area, probability calibration or first-shot score.
For example `[0,87.5,87.5,87.5]` gives 65.625%, while four 87.5% points give 87.5%.
Both can end at the same legal-space maximum. Existing model regressions and
slower initial progress remain visible.

These are six reused bilingual views over three structural templates, repeated
across five arms, three execution modes and two passes. They are not 180 new
independent tasks. Original UNKNOWN CI context, weights and earlier evidence stay
unchanged. The new summary is a separate artifact; it does not rewrite the frozen
cost report, raw NDJSON, case results or Hugging Face model card.

Next bounded work can compare partial curves under smaller budgets and new
interacting structures, with group-separated Korean/English paraphrases and
explicit ambiguous intentions. Where legal-space enumeration is incomplete,
report the legal maximum and irreducible gap as unknown; a best observed candidate
is only a lower bound. Any training phase needs its own frozen curriculum and
compute record. Optimizing iterative progress must account for model-call, CPU,
memory and preparation costs alongside finite completion.
